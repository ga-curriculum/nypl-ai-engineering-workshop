# Exercise 2: Map Your Practices to NYPL's AI Policy

## Duration: 8 minutes (Stretch activity)

## Purpose
Compare the generic 5-essentials governance framework against NYPL's real AI Principles & Working Staff Guidelines, and identify how your day-to-day engineering practices align.

## Reference

> **Note:** This exercise references NYPL's internal AI Principles & Working Staff Guidelines. The detailed reference is available in `slides/nypl-ai-guidelines-CONFIDENTIAL.md` — for workshop use only, not for distribution.

NYPL's policy covers three layers:
- **Core Objectives:** Empower through education, enhance (not replace), advance library-centric solutions
- **Guiding Principles:** Human-first approach, accountability, equity, security, privacy, transparency, accuracy, ethical conduct, digital literacy, IP protection, sustainability
- **Working Guidelines:** Approved tools only, review/revise/fact-check, label AI work, no unapproved systems, no deception, no AI-generated public-facing media

## Instructions

### Step 1: Map the 5 essentials to NYPL's policy (5 min)

For each essential, identify where NYPL's policy already provides guidance:

**1. Approved Tools**
- NYPL says: Use only pre-approved AI systems vetted by IT/Digital
- How does this apply to your coding tools (Copilot, Gemini Code Assist, etc.)?
- Are the tools you used today on the approved list?

**2. Disclosure Requirements**
- NYPL says: Label all work outputs largely or fully generated by AI
- How does this translate to PRs, commits, and code comments?
- Does the disclosure spectrum from the slides align with NYPL's labeling requirement?

**3. Review Standards**
- NYPL says: Review, revise, and fact-check all AI-generated material
- How does the QA Framework (Generate > Review > Refine) satisfy this requirement?
- Does your current code review process already cover this?

**4. Data Handling**
- NYPL says: Don't use unapproved systems (data could be exposed); uphold security and privacy
- What code or data should you avoid pasting into AI tools?
- Are there NYPL-specific restrictions on patron data, internal systems, or proprietary code?

**5. Accountability**
- NYPL says: An individual or group always remains responsible for AI-generated work
- The engineer who commits the code owns it — how does your team enforce this?
- What happens when AI-generated code causes a production incident?

### Step 2: Identify gaps (3 min)

Review your mapping and identify:
- Where does NYPL's policy already answer your questions clearly?
- Where do you need more specific guidance for engineering workflows?
- What would you raise with your team lead or the AI Working Group?
- Does anything in the "What NOT To Do" list change how you'd use AI tools day-to-day?

## Share Out

Be prepared to share: **What's one NYPL principle that will change how you use AI coding tools going forward?**
